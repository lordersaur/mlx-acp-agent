**3.2. Safety and Harmlessness:**

**3.4. Honesty about Limitations:**

# 🧠 7. ADVANCED PROMPT ENGINEERING FOR GEMMA 4
================================================================================

To maximize the utility and precision of Gemma 4, users should move beyond simple queries and employ advanced prompt engineering techniques. These methods guide the model toward highly specific, high-quality outputs.

**7.1. Role Prompting (Persona Assignment):**
Assigning a specific persona or role to the model dramatically improves the quality and tone of the response.
*Example:* Instead of "Write about climate change," use "Act as a leading climate scientist presenting a briefing to a panel of government officials. Detail the economic and social impacts of rising sea levels."

**7.2. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:**
For complex reasoning tasks (math, logic, multi-step planning), instruct the model to show its work. This forces the model to break down the problem internally before providing the final answer, significantly improving accuracy.
*Instruction:* "Before providing the final answer, first outline the steps you will take to solve this problem. Then, execute those steps sequentially."

**7.3. Few-Shot Learning:**
Provide the model with several examples of the desired input-output format before asking the main question. This teaches the model the specific style, structure, and constraints required for the task.
*Example:* If you want a summary in a specific bulleted format, provide 2-3 examples of source text and the desired summary format.

**7.4. Constraint Definition and Delimiters:**
Clearly define all constraints (word count, tone, required keywords, forbidden topics) and use delimiters (e.g., triple quotes, XML tags) to separate instructions from the input data. This prevents the model from confusing instructions with content.
*Example:* "Summarize the text enclosed in <TEXT> tags. The summary must be under 150 words and maintain a highly formal tone."
